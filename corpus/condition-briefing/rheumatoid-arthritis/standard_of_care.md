Rheumatoid arthritis care follows treat-to-target: disease activity is measured regularly, and therapy is
adjusted until remission or low disease activity is reached.

Methotrexate is the usual first-line disease-modifying drug for rheumatoid arthritis, started early after
diagnosis, often with short-term low-dose glucocorticoids as a bridge.

When rheumatoid arthritis stays active on methotrexate, a biologic (such as a TNF inhibitor) or a JAK inhibitor
is added, with JAK inhibitor choice weighing cardiovascular and other safety risks.

Rheumatoid arthritis patients on disease-modifying drugs are screened for infections before treatment, kept up to
date on vaccines, and monitored with periodic blood tests.

Rheumatoid arthritis care also includes physical and occupational therapy, cardiovascular risk management, and
attention to bone health.
