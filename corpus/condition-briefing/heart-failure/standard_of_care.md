Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) is treated with four medication classes started early and
titrated together: an ARNI (or ACE inhibitor/ARB), an evidence-based beta blocker, a mineralocorticoid receptor
antagonist, and an SGLT2 inhibitor.

Loop diuretics relieve congestion in heart failure, but they treat symptoms and are adjusted to fluid status
rather than used to change long-term outcomes.

For heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF), SGLT2 inhibitors are part of standard therapy, along
with diuretics for congestion and treatment of contributors such as hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and obesity.

Selected heart failure patients with reduced ejection fraction receive devices: an implantable
cardioverter-defibrillator for sudden-death prevention, and cardiac resynchronization therapy when the QRS is wide.

Heart failure care also includes cardiac rehabilitation, sodium and fluid guidance, structured follow-up after
hospital discharge, and, in advanced disease, mechanical support, transplant evaluation, or palliative care.
