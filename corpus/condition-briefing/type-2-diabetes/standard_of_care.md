Type 2 diabetes care starts with lifestyle change: nutrition therapy, physical activity, and weight management
are recommended for every patient and continue alongside any medication.

Metformin remains a common first-line glucose-lowering drug for type 2 diabetes when there is no
contraindication, because it is effective, inexpensive, and has a long safety record.

In type 2 diabetes with established cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney disease, current
practice adds an SGLT2 inhibitor or a GLP-1 receptor agonist for organ protection, largely independent of the
glucose level.

Glycemic targets in type 2 diabetes are individualized. An HbA1c around 7% is a common goal for many adults,
with looser targets for older patients or those at risk of hypoglycemia.

Type 2 diabetes care also covers cardiovascular risk: blood pressure control, statin therapy for most adults,
and smoking cessation, plus routine screening for eye, kidney, and foot complications.

When oral and injectable non-insulin therapies do not reach target, basal insulin is added for type 2
diabetes, with dose adjustment guided by fasting glucose.
