Stroke prevention is the first decision in atrial fibrillation: stroke risk is scored (for example with
CHA2DS2-VASc) and weighed against bleeding risk to decide on anticoagulation.

Direct oral anticoagulants are preferred over warfarin for most atrial fibrillation patients. Warfarin remains the
choice with mechanical heart valves or moderate-to-severe mitral stenosis.

Atrial fibrillation symptoms are managed with rate control (beta blockers or non-dihydropyridine calcium channel
blockers) or rhythm control (antiarrhythmic drugs or cardioversion).

Catheter ablation is an established rhythm-control option in atrial fibrillation, increasingly offered early in
symptomatic patients and in selected patients with heart failure.

Atrial fibrillation care also targets risk factors that drive recurrence: weight, blood pressure, sleep apnea,
alcohol intake, and physical activity.
