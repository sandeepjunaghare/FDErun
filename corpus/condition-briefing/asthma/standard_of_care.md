Asthma treatment in adults and adolescents is built on inhaled corticosteroids, because they reduce airway
inflammation and the risk of severe attacks.

A common approach in asthma uses an ICS-formoterol inhaler both as the reliever and, at higher steps, as the
maintenance treatment, rather than relying on a short-acting reliever alone.

Asthma therapy is stepped up or down by symptom control and exacerbation history, after checking inhaler
technique, adherence, and triggers.

Severe asthma that stays uncontrolled on high-dose inhaled therapy is referred to a specialist for phenotyping,
and many patients with type 2 inflammation receive biologic therapy.

Every asthma patient should have a written action plan, and care includes managing allergies, smoking exposure,
and other conditions that worsen control.
