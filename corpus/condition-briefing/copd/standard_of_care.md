COPD is confirmed by spirometry showing persistent airflow limitation (a post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC ratio below
0.7), and treatment is chosen by symptom burden and exacerbation history.

Smoking cessation is the single most effective step in COPD care and is offered at every visit, with counseling
and medication.

Long-acting bronchodilators are the foundation of COPD maintenance therapy: a LAMA, a LABA, or a LAMA/LABA
combination for patients with more symptoms.

An inhaled corticosteroid is added in COPD mainly for patients with frequent exacerbations and raised blood
eosinophil counts, because that is where the benefit outweighs the pneumonia risk.

Non-drug COPD care includes pulmonary rehabilitation, vaccinations, inhaler technique checks, and long-term
oxygen for patients with severe resting hypoxemia.
