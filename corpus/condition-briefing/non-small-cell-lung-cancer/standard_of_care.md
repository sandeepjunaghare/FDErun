Non-small cell lung cancer treatment is driven by stage, so staging with imaging and tissue sampling comes first,
reviewed by a multidisciplinary tumor board.

Early-stage non-small cell lung cancer is treated with surgery where possible, and stereotactic radiation is an
option for patients who cannot have surgery. Selected patients receive immunotherapy or targeted therapy before or
after surgery.

Unresectable stage III non-small cell lung cancer is commonly treated with chemoradiation followed by
consolidation immunotherapy.

In advanced non-small cell lung cancer, broad biomarker testing (for example EGFR, ALK, and PD-L1) guides first-line
choice between targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and chemotherapy combinations.

Non-small cell lung cancer care also includes lung cancer screening with low-dose CT for eligible high-risk adults,
smoking cessation, and early palliative care in advanced disease.
