Major depressive disorder is diagnosed by clinical assessment, often supported by a standardized questionnaire
such as the PHQ-9, which is also used to track response.

For mild-to-moderate major depressive disorder, evidence-based psychotherapy such as cognitive behavioral therapy
and antidepressant medication are both first-line options, alone or together.

SSRIs and SNRIs are common first-choice antidepressants for major depressive disorder, chosen by side-effect
profile, prior response, and other conditions.

When major depressive disorder does not respond to two adequate antidepressant trials, options include switching,
augmentation, esketamine nasal spray, and electroconvulsive therapy for severe or treatment-resistant illness.

Major depressive disorder care includes suicide risk assessment at every stage, measurement-based follow-up, and
integration of behavioral health into primary care.
